# Story content editorial QA

All three passages were written originally for DailyLingo in this change; no third-party story text or live translation service is used. This is an engineering/editorial review, not a claim of external CEFR certification or a human professional translator review.

| Story | Target | Sentences | Language / pedagogy | Vietnamese review |

| The Little Blue Bag | A1 | 12 | Concrete park/friendship setting; present simple, possessives, prepositions; short clauses | Reviewed sentence by sentence; pronouns consistently refer to Mai and the boy |

Each sentence has a local authored translation, grammatical explanation in Vietnamese, and a separate illustrative English example. English appears first; translations are learner-controlled in the reader. Grammar notes describe the sentence actually shown and do not present the example as story text.

Glossary IPA is broad General American; pronunciation can differ from the user's selected browser voice. Six contextual senses per story are explicitly curated. Remaining words must not be assigned invented dictionary definitions. CEFR levels describe intended reading difficulty with support; familiar content words and context can still require lookup.

Editorial word IDs represent senses, with exact case-sensitive token ranges in each sentence. The same sense ID marks repeated occurrences in a story; “apple” is deliberately not “apples”. Inflections or another sense require a separate editorial entry. No substring or lowercase matching drives marks. Cross-story notebook matching must include part of speech and Vietnamese meaning in addition to spelling, or use a validated explicit notebook identity.

Sentence IDs are permanent content identifiers. Adding/reordering text in a future revision requires preserving IDs of unchanged sentences, allocating new IDs for new sentences, and incrementing version. The preparation splitter is deterministic across runtimes, preserves punctuation and quotation marks, recognises common titles/initials, and supports explicit end-offset corrections for dialogue attribution or unusual abbreviations. Paragraph structure is authored and preserved; source reconstruction and exact token surfaces are tested.
